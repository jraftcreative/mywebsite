A client is sitting on the MRT, scrolling through her phone. Her regular stylist has moved overseas and she needs someone new. A year ago she would have searched Google Maps or asked her group chat. Today she opens ChatGPT and types:

"Recommend a Korean hair salon near Orchard that's good with fine hair."

Other prompts look like these. "Gel nails in Tampines open on Sunday." "Lash lift for sensitive eyes, Singapore." "Best place for a brow lamination near Paya Lebar."

The AI replies with three or four salon names. Those salons get the booking. They aren't always the biggest or the best-known ones. They're the ones whose information AI can actually find, read and trust.

This guide explains how that choice gets made, and what a Singapore salon can do this month to be part of it. It covers hair, nails, lashes, brows, waxing, non-medical facials and spa services. Aesthetic clinics work under MOH advertising rules and need a different approach.

## How are Singapore clients using AI to choose a salon?

They use AI to shortlist before they book. Instead of scrolling through dozens of Maps results, clients describe exactly what they want (service, area, hair type, budget, opening hours) and ask AI to narrow it down. The salons named in that answer become the shortlist, and the rest are rarely considered.

The numbers back this up. In a May 2026 [impact.com survey](https://impact.com/infographics/singapore-shopping-ai-trends-info-imp-aw-sea/) of 1,092 Singapore consumers who already use AI tools, two in three had used ChatGPT, and **39% said they rely on AI for health, beauty and wellness recommendations**. That's the third-highest category, behind only travel and electronics.

Google is moving the same way. AI Overviews now appear on roughly half of all searches, so even clients who never open ChatGPT are reading an AI-written summary before they see a single salon listing.

For salons this matters more than for most businesses, because beauty questions are so specific. Nobody asks "find me a salon". They ask for a particular service, in a particular area, for a particular need. That's exactly the kind of question AI is good at answering, if it has the details to work with.

## What does AI look at before it recommends a salon?

AI recommends the salons it can describe with confidence. It looks for consistent, specific, recent information across several sources: your Google Business Profile, your reviews, your website and your booking listings. When those sources agree and are detailed, AI can match you to a precise question. When they're thin or contradictory, it picks someone else.

In practice, these are the signals that count most.

**Reviews, and what they say.** Volume helps, but recency and content matter just as much. Fifty reviews from last year saying "great service!" tell AI very little. Twenty recent reviews mentioning "balayage", "fine hair" and "Bugis" tell it a lot.

**A complete Google Business Profile.** Google's own AI draws directly from it. Services with prices, accurate opening hours (including public holidays), the right categories and recent photos all feed the answer.

**A clear speciality.** "Hair salon" is generic. "Korean perms and colour correction for fine hair" is a match. AI rewards salons that state clearly what they do best.

**Consistent details everywhere.** Your salon name, address and phone number should read exactly the same on your website, Google, Fresha and every other listing. Small mismatches, like an old unit number or a former name, make AI less sure it's looking at the same business.

**Pages it can actually read.** A website with a page for each core service, listing what's included, how long it takes and a starting price, gives AI facts it can quote.

## Why isn't Instagram enough on its own?

Because AI tools can't see most of it. Much of Instagram sits behind a login and isn't easily read by AI crawlers, so the portfolio, captions and comments a salon has spent years building are largely invisible to ChatGPT. Instagram builds trust once a client finds you. It rarely gets you found by AI in the first place.

This is the biggest gap we see with Singapore salons. Many run their whole business through Instagram and WhatsApp. The work is beautiful, the reviews are in the comments, and prices are "DM for rates". To an AI model, that salon barely exists.

You don't need to post less on Instagram. You need the key facts (services, prices, location, hours, speciality) to also live somewhere AI can read: your website and your Google Business Profile.

## Six steps to get your salon recommended by AI

### 1. Fill in every part of your Google Business Profile

List each service with a short description and a starting price. Set accurate hours, including Sundays and public holidays, since "open on Sunday" is a common question. Choose a precise primary category (Nail Salon, Hair Salon, Eyelash Service) rather than a broad one. Add new photos of your work and your space every month.

### 2. Get reviews that mention the service and the neighbourhood

Ask happy clients for a review while they're still in the chair, and make it easy with a QR code at the counter. You can say, "If you mention which service you had, it really helps other clients find us." Never script reviews or offer discounts in exchange for them. Google's policies ban incentivised reviews, and a wave of reviews that sound alike does more harm than good. Reply to every review, briefly and personally.

### 3. Give each core service its own page

One page each for colour, perms, nails, lashes and so on, depending on what you offer. Each page should say what the service is, who it suits, how long it takes, aftercare, and a starting price or range. These pages give AI something specific to match against "how much is a keratin treatment in Singapore" or "lash lift for sensitive eyes". If you're not sure your site is set up to do this, our guide to [salon websites](/beauty-salon-website-singapore/) covers the structure.

### 4. Keep your booking listings consistent

If you take bookings through Fresha or a similar platform, make sure the services, prices and details there match your website and Google. Listings on established platforms are third-party confirmation that you are who you say you are, and AI uses that to decide how much to trust you.

### 5. Add structured data and an llms.txt file

Structured data (schema markup) is code on your website that labels your business details in a format machines read without guessing: business type, address, hours, services and prices. An llms.txt file is a short plain-text summary at the root of your site that tells AI tools what matters most about your salon. Neither is visible to clients, and both make you easier to cite. Our [guide to what GEO is](/blog/what-is-geo-ai-search-optimisation/) explains how these fit together.

### 6. Answer the questions clients really ask

Collect the questions you hear every week. "Can I colour my hair after rebonding?" "How long do gel extensions last?" "Do you do lashes for sensitive eyes?" Answer them on an FAQ page in clear, short paragraphs. These are almost word for word the questions clients type into AI, and a salon that answers them clearly becomes the source the AI quotes.

## What stops AI from recommending a salon?

Usually it's small gaps, not big failures. Outdated hours, prices hidden behind "DM us", a Linktree instead of a website, or a recent move that was never updated everywhere. Each one makes AI less certain about your salon, and an uncertain AI simply names someone else.

These are the ones we see most often:

- **"DM for rates."** Clients ask AI about price constantly. With no published price, you can't be matched to any of those questions.
- **Hours that don't match.** Google says you close at 8pm, your website says 9pm, Fresha says 7pm. AI notices, and it trusts all three less.
- **A link page instead of a website.** A Linktree or a single booking link gives AI almost nothing to read about your services or speciality.
- **An old address still floating around.** If you've moved, every listing needs updating, including the old ones you've forgotten about.
- **Reviews left unanswered.** Replies show the business is active and looked after, which counts for both clients and AI.

## Prompts to test your salon's AI visibility

Run these in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode, swapping in your own services and neighbourhood:

- "Recommend a good [service] salon in [neighbourhood], Singapore."
- "Best salon for [specific need, e.g. fine hair / sensitive eyes] in Singapore."
- "[Service] near [MRT station] open on Sunday."
- "How much does [service] cost in Singapore? Which salons are good value?"
- "Which salons in [area] have the best reviews for [service]?"
- "Tell me about [your salon name]."

For each prompt, note three things. Are you named at all? Is your website linked? And who is named instead? That last list is your real competition in AI search, and it's often different from the salons you think of as rivals. Run the same prompts every month so you can see whether things are moving.

The last prompt matters more than it looks. If AI gets your hours, location or services wrong when asked about you directly, it won't confidently recommend you for anything else.

## The salons that will win AI search

They won't necessarily have the biggest budgets or the most followers. They'll be the ones whose information is specific, current and the same everywhere: clear services, published starting prices, a steady flow of reviews that mention the work, and a website that answers real questions.

That's good news for independent salons. A focused salon with a clear speciality and 80 genuine, detailed reviews can be named ahead of a chain with a generic profile. Specific beats big in AI search.
