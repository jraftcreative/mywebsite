If you run a small business in Singapore, you've probably had this thought: is a marketing agency actually worth it — for someone my size?

Fair question. Most agency marketing is built for big brands with big budgets. And you've heard the stories: a business pays a monthly retainer, gets pretty reports and a few hundred new followers, and ends the year with no more customers than it started with. When you're watching every dollar, that's not a hypothetical risk. It's the reason you've been putting off the decision.

So we won't try to convince you that agencies are worth it. We'll just show you what a good one actually does for a small business. Here's one of ours.

## A one-person business, up against schools

Music with Pat is a private music teacher in Singapore. One person. She teaches piano, violin, ABRSM theory and adult lessons, and she's genuinely good at it. But being good at teaching doesn't fill your week with students. She was competing for attention against music schools that have front-desk staff, marketing people, and budgets she couldn't touch.

What she wanted was simple, and it's probably what you want too: a steady flow of the right people booking a trial lesson. Not likes. Not reach. Trial lessons, because a trial is where a new student relationship actually begins.

That was the brief she gave Jraft Creative. It's the brief we built everything around.

## What "results" actually looked like

We run Music with Pat's Google Ads, and we point every part of them at one outcome: a booked trial lesson from a parent or adult learner who's genuinely looking for lessons. Every search we go after, every dollar of a modest budget, aimed at that.

Over three months, that produced **45 booked trial lessons. Around 15 a month.** On a small budget.

Let us be straight about what that number is and isn't. It's 45 real bookings: parents and adult learners who found her, chose her, and put a trial in the calendar. It isn't a vanity figure dressed up to look good. And we're not going to quote you a shiny "return on ad spend," because a trial lesson is counted as a booking, not a dollar amount, so any ROAS figure would be invented. What we can stand behind is the thing that matters to a music teacher: her diary filled up, month after month, quietly in the background while she taught.

For a solo teacher, that's the difference between hoping the phone rings and knowing new students are on the way.

## The part that applies to you

Music with Pat isn't a big brand. She's a small business on a modest budget, same as most of Singapore. What made her marketing work wasn't a bigger spend or a cleverer logo. It was focus. Every decision aimed at the one action that pays her, and nothing wasted on the numbers that don't.

That's the real test of a marketing agency for a small business. Not how polished the reports are. Not how many followers you gained last month. One question: did more of the right customers actually come?

So if you're comparing agencies right now, ask each of them the same things:

- **What will you measure** — bookings and enquiries, or impressions and likes?
- **Will you take a modest budget seriously,** or is a small business not worth your attention?
- **Can you point to a real result for a business my size** — not a Fortune 500 case study that has nothing to do with a studio, a clinic, or a shop in Singapore?

If an agency can't answer those plainly, keep looking.
