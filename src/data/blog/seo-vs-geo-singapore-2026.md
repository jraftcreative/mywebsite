Every few weeks a Singapore business owner asks us some version of the same question: *"Do I still need SEO, or is it all about AI now?"* It usually arrives after they've read a breathless article claiming Google search is dead and GEO is the only thing that matters.

Here's the honest answer, and it's not the one that sells the most services: **SEO and GEO are not an either/or.** They're two halves of how people find you now, they lean on the same foundations, and the real question isn't *which one* — it's *in what order, and at what split, for a business like yours.*

This is the version we'd give a friend. No hype, no doom.

## SEO and GEO, in plain English

**SEO — Search Engine Optimisation** — is the work that gets your website to rank on Google's classic results: the blue links, the map pack, the "people also ask" boxes. Someone types *"aircon servicing Bishan"*, they scroll, they click a result, they land on your site. SEO is about earning that click.

**GEO — Generative Engine Optimisation** — is the newer discipline. It's the work that gets your brand *named and recommended inside an AI-generated answer*: ChatGPT, Gemini, Google's AI Overview, Perplexity. Someone asks ChatGPT *"who does good aircon servicing near Bishan?"* and the AI writes a paragraph that names three companies. GEO is about being one of those three.

The distinction that trips people up: with SEO you're competing for a *position in a list of links*. With GEO you're competing to be *mentioned in a sentence*. Different mechanic, different signals, often different winners for the same query. (We dug into why the two rarely overlap in [how AI models decide which brands to recommend](/blog/how-chatgpt-decides-which-brands-to-recommend/).)

## What each is good at — and where each falls short

**SEO is good at** capturing people who already know roughly what they want and are ready to click. It's mature, measurable, and the intent is high: someone searching *"quotation for office renovation Singapore"* is close to buying. It also compounds — a page that ranks keeps working for years.

**Where SEO falls short:** the first screen of Google is increasingly crowded — ads, map pack, AI Overview — and the classic blue links have been pushed down. Ranking well no longer guarantees the visibility it did five years ago. And SEO does nothing for the growing number of buyers who never open Google at all and just ask an AI.

**GEO is good at** capturing the shift in how people research. When someone asks an AI assistant for a recommendation, being named puts you in front of a buyer who arrives *pre-sold* — a trusted assistant vouched for you, so there's less scepticism than a cold click. In Singapore, one of the highest AI-adoption markets in the world, this is already happening, not a someday thing. We've had it happen to us directly: a client of ours, Maple Bear, first discovered Jraft Creative through ChatGPT — the AI named us, and that's how the conversation started.

**Where GEO falls short:** it's newer, the answers are probabilistic (an AI can name you today and not tomorrow), and it's harder to measure than a rank tracker. It also can't stand on its own — GEO leans on the same technical and content foundations SEO needs, which is exactly why the sequencing matters.

## Why GEO matters *now* in Singapore

The reason this isn't a "wait and see" topic: discovery is genuinely moving. Google's AI Overviews — the AI-written summary that now sits at the top of many searches — reached roughly **2.5 billion monthly users** as of Google's own 2026 announcement. That's not a niche feature; it's the default experience for a huge share of searches. When an AI answer occupies the top of the page and names a few brands, those brands get the first read, the first click, the first enquiry.

And crucially, being cited in that AI answer is a *different game* from ranking below it. You can sit at position one on classic Google and still be absent from the AI Overview, because the two systems weigh different signals. So a Singapore business that has "done SEO" and stopped there may be quietly invisible in exactly the place discovery is heading.

That's the opportunity. The supply side hasn't caught up — most Singapore businesses have no GEO strategy at all. The gap is the window.

## The honest framework: how to decide the split

Here's where most agencies pick a side — either "SEO is dead, buy GEO" or "GEO is hype, buy SEO." Both are wrong, and usually self-serving. Our position: **they're complementary, foundations come first, and the right split depends on the business.** Here's how we actually decide.

### Foundations first — always

Before you split any budget, one truth holds for everyone: **GEO is built on top of the same foundations as SEO.** Clean technical structure, structured data (schema), a complete and accurate Google Business Profile, and specific, citable content — AI engines read all of these to decide whether to trust and name you. There is no "skip SEO, do GEO" shortcut, because the things that make an AI cite you are largely the things that make Google rank you.

So the first slice of budget isn't labelled SEO or GEO. It's *foundations* — the work both depend on. Get that wrong and you're paying for AI visibility on a site the AI can't parse.

### Then split by four factors

**1. Buyer behaviour in your category.** Do your customers Google and click, or do they ask around (increasingly, ask an AI)? High-consideration, research-heavy purchases — clinics, B2B services, education, financial advice — are exactly where people now ask AI for a shortlist. Lean GEO. Fast, transactional, local intent — *"plumber near me now"* — still lives mostly on classic search and the map pack. Lean SEO.

**2. Business type.** A local service business living or dying on the Google map pack should nail SEO and Google Business Profile first. A considered-purchase or reputation-led business — where being *recommended* matters more than being *found in a list* — should weight GEO more heavily.

**3. Budget.** On a tight budget, don't split thin across both. Fund foundations, then the one channel your buyers actually use. Spreading a small budget across two disciplines usually means neither reaches the threshold that produces results.

**4. Timeline.** Both are slow-burn — neither is a two-week fix. If you need enquiries *this month*, that's a paid-ads conversation, not SEO or GEO. Set the expectation honestly: earning your way into either classic rankings or AI answers is a multi-quarter effort.

For most Singapore SMEs we work with, the practical shape is: **foundations first, then a roughly 60/40 lean toward whichever channel their buyers use most — with the whole thing built so the SEO work simultaneously feeds GEO.** Because when the foundations are right, one investment compounds across both.

## Where you probably are right now

Most Singapore businesses sit in one of three places:

- **Solid classic SEO, invisible in AI answers.** You rank, but the AI Overview and ChatGPT don't name you. The fix is GEO on top of foundations you mostly already have — often the fastest win.
- **Neither working.** Thin site, no schema, incomplete Google Business Profile. Start at foundations; they serve both channels at once.
- **Chasing GEO with no foundation.** You've read the hype and want AI visibility, but the underlying site can't support it. This is the most common trap — and the most wasteful.

Knowing which one you're in is the whole decision. It's a 20-minute conversation, not a leap of faith.
