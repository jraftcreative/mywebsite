When someone in Singapore needs a service *right now* — a dentist in Tampines, a hair salon near their office, a plumber before the weekend — they don't scroll a page of blue links. They open Google, and the first thing they see is Google Maps — a little map with three businesses pinned under it. That box is called the **map pack**, and for a huge share of local searches in Singapore, it's where the decision actually happens.

Being one of those three is worth more than almost any other ranking a local business can win. So here's the plain-English version of how Google decides who makes the map pack in Singapore — the levers you genuinely control, and the honest bit about the ones you don't.

## First, what "ranking on Google Maps" actually means

There are two places your business can show up when someone searches locally, and they're linked but not identical.

**The map pack** (also called the local pack) is the map-plus-three-listings box at the top of a normal Google search — *"cafe near me,"* *"aircon servicing Bishan."* It's prime real estate because it sits above the classic blue links and it's built for people ready to act: call, get directions, book.

**Google Maps itself** is the app people open to search directly — *"orthodontist Serangoon"* — and scroll a longer ranked list.

Both are powered by the same thing: your **Google Business Profile** (the free listing you claim for your business). Optimise the profile well, and you improve your standing in both at once. Ignore it, and you're invisible in the exact place local customers decide.

## Why this is the highest-ROI ranking a local business can chase

For a business with a physical location or a local service area, the map pack beats almost everything else, for three reasons.

**The intent is red-hot.** Someone searching *"nail salon near me"* isn't researching for next month — they want an appointment today. Map pack traffic converts to calls, walk-ins and bookings faster than nearly any other channel.

**It's free to appear.** Unlike ads, you don't pay per click to sit in the map pack. You earn the spot. That makes it one of the best returns on effort available to a Singapore SME.

**Most competitors do it badly.** The bar is low. A huge number of Singapore businesses have a half-finished Google Business Profile — wrong category, no photos, unanswered reviews. Doing it properly is often enough to leapfrog them.

## How Google decides who ranks — its three factors

Google is unusually open about this. Local ranking comes down to three things: **relevance, distance, and prominence.** Understanding which you can move is the whole game.

**1. Relevance** — how well your business matches what was searched. This is largely about your Google Business Profile: your primary category, the services you list, and the information on your profile and website. A dental clinic that has set its category to "Dental clinic" (not just "Doctor") and lists "braces," "teeth whitening," "wisdom tooth extraction" will match far more searches than one with a vague category and no service list. **You control this fully.**

**2. Distance** — how far your business is from the searcher (or the location they typed). If someone in Jurong searches "physiotherapy," Google leans toward physiotherapists near Jurong. **You mostly can't control this** — you are where you are — which is exactly why the other two factors matter so much. You can't move your shopfront, but you can out-optimise a closer competitor on relevance and prominence and still win the slot.

**3. Prominence** — how well-known and trusted your business is. This is driven by **reviews** (quantity, quality, recency, and whether you respond), mentions of your business across the web, consistent listings in local directories, and your website's overall authority. Prominence is where most of the real, ongoing work lives — and where you can genuinely pull ahead.

## The levers that actually move the needle

Here's what to do, in rough order of impact, for a Singapore business.

### Claim and fully complete your Google Business Profile

The single biggest miss we see. Every field filled: correct name, address, phone (your real local number), website, opening hours *including public holidays* — Singapore has a lot of them, and wrong holiday hours frustrate customers and signal neglect to Google.

### Get your primary category exactly right

Your **primary category** is one of the strongest relevance signals there is. Be specific — "Ramen restaurant," not "Restaurant"; "Aesthetic clinic," not "Medical clinic." Then add relevant secondary categories. Guessing this wrong quietly caps how many searches you can ever appear for.

### Make reviews a system, not an afterthought

Reviews are the heaviest prominence lever, and the most under-worked. Aim for a steady stream, not a one-off burst — Google notices *recency*. Ask happy customers at the moment they're happiest (after the service, on the receipt, via a follow-up message). And respond to *every* review, good or bad, within a day or two: it signals an active, real business and it's a ranking factor in its own right. One hard rule — **never buy fake reviews.** Google detects and penalises them, and in Singapore's tight communities it's a reputational risk that isn't worth it.

### Keep your NAP identical everywhere

**NAP** = Name, Address, Phone. It must be *character-for-character identical* across your website, Google Business Profile, Facebook, and every directory you're listed on. "Blk 123" in one place and "Block 123" in another is the kind of inconsistency that makes Google less confident it's dealing with one real business. Consistency builds trust; mismatches erode it.

### Add real photos, regularly

Profiles with genuine, current photos — exterior, interior, team, products — get more views and clicks, and the engagement feeds prominence. Real beats stock every time; customers (and Google) can tell.

### Back it with local content on your website

Your profile and your website work together. Service pages that clearly state what you do and *where* you serve, plus location-specific content, reinforce relevance. This is where local SEO and the rest of your [SEO foundations](/blog/seo-mistakes-singapore-businesses/) meet — the same work that helps you rank on Maps helps you rank in classic search, and increasingly helps AI tools recommend you too.

## The honest part — what to expect

No tricks, so let's be straight about the limits.

**Distance is a real ceiling.** If a customer is closer to a competitor, you may not outrank them for that specific search no matter what — and that's fine. Your job is to win the searches where you're genuinely a strong, nearby option, and to out-work closer competitors on relevance and reputation so you're in the mix more often than your postcode alone would earn.

**It's a slow burn, not a switch.** Completing your profile can help within weeks, but prominence — the review stream, the consistent signals, the authority — builds over months. Anyone promising you the top of the map pack "in days" is guessing or fibbing.

**It needs maintenance.** Hours change, you add a service, reviews come in. A profile you set up once and abandon slides. The businesses that win treat it as a living thing.

## Where you probably are right now

Most Singapore businesses we look at sit in one of three places:

- **Unclaimed or barely filled profile.** The most common — and the fastest win, because there's so much easy ground to make up.
- **Claimed but stale.** Set up once, then left. Wrong category, thin photos, unanswered reviews. Real gains available from a proper tune-up.
- **Solid profile, weak prominence.** The profile's fine, but reviews and local authority lag competitors. This is where the ongoing work pays off.

Knowing which one you're in is the whole decision.
