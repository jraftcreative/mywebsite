## The Short Answer

Yes — for almost every Singapore business, you still need a website in 2026, even if Instagram is where most of your activity happens. Not because Instagram isn't working, but because the two do completely different jobs, and social media quietly caps how far you can grow on its own.

There's a narrow set of cases where social-only is genuinely fine for now, and we'll be honest about those below. But if you sell a service, take bookings, or anyone ever googles your name before buying, a website isn't optional — it's the piece that turns attention into customers.

## Why "Instagram Is Enough" Feels True (Until It Isn't)

Plenty of Singapore businesses launch on Instagram, get some traction, and conclude they don't need anything else. For a while, that holds. You post, people DM, some buy. No hosting, no build, no fuss.

The problem shows up later, and quietly. You're building your entire business on land you don't own. Instagram decides who sees your posts, how your DMs get filtered, and — in a bad week — whether your account stays up at all. People lose years of audience to a hack or a wrongful ban with no way to appeal. When the platform changes the rules, your reach changes with it, and you have no say.

A website is the one piece of your online presence you actually own. That alone is worth the build.

## What a Website Does That Instagram Can't

Three things, and each one is a gap social media can't fill.

**It gets you found on Google.** When someone searches "florist Tiong Bahru" or "physiotherapist near me," Instagram pages rarely show up — websites do. A huge share of buying in Singapore starts with a Google search, and if you're not there, you don't exist for that customer. We wrote about this exact problem in [why your business isn't showing up on Google](/blog/why-not-showing-up-on-google-singapore/).

**It lets you own the relationship.** On Instagram you rent access to your followers. A website lets you capture enquiries, take bookings, and build a contact list that's yours — so you can reach customers directly instead of hoping the algorithm shows them your post.

**It controls the path to buying.** A grid makes someone piece your story together from squares and captions. A website walks them through it: what you offer, why you're credible, proof it works, and one clear way to take the next step. That structure is what a [good business website](/blog/good-business-website-singapore/) is built to do, and it's where casual interest becomes a real enquiry.

## The Trust Gap Nobody Mentions

Here's the pattern almost every Singaporean follows for anything beyond a cheap impulse buy: see it on social or hear about it from a friend, then google the business to check it's legit.

If that search turns up a proper website — clear, professional, answers the obvious questions — you've passed the test. If it turns up only a thin Instagram page with a few posts and no detail, a small doubt creeps in. For a S$15 accessory, that doubt doesn't matter. For a S$3,000 renovation, a wedding photographer, or a clinic, it's often the whole decision.

A website signals permanence. It says you're a real business that'll still be here next year, not an account that might vanish. For higher-value or considered purchases, that signal does a lot of quiet selling.

## But Isn't a Link-in-Bio or Facebook Page Enough?

These feel like a website, which is why so many businesses stop at them. They're not, and the gaps matter.

A **link-in-bio page** (Linktree and the like) is just a list of links. It doesn't rank on Google, it holds none of your actual content, and it still lives inside the same rented ecosystem. It's a signpost, not a destination — useful alongside a site, no substitute for one.

A **Facebook page** has the same core problem as Instagram: you don't own it, it barely surfaces in Google search for what you sell, and its reach gets throttled unless you pay. It's another discovery channel, not a home base.

**Marketplaces** like Carousell or a delivery-app listing can drive real sales, but they drop you in a crowded aisle next to every competitor, take a cut, and own the customer — not you. Good for volume, poor for building a brand people seek out by name.

The thread running through all of these: they're platforms you borrow, tuned for the platform's goals, not yours. A website is the one place online that works entirely for your business — found on Google, owned outright, and built to convert the way you want. Everything else should point *to* it, not stand in for it.

## When Social-Only Is Actually Fine (For Now)

We'd rather be honest than sell you something you don't need yet. You can reasonably run on Instagram alone if:

- You're **just testing an idea** and not ready to invest in anything permanent.
- You sell **low-cost, visual, impulse-buy products** where people decide in seconds and check out inside the app.
- It's a **hobby or side project** where growth isn't the goal.

Even then, the moment you want to be found on Google, take bookings, look credible to a cautious buyer, or stop depending on an algorithm for every sale — you've outgrown social-only. For most businesses, that moment comes faster than expected.

## The Real Answer: They Work Together

This was never website *versus* Instagram. The businesses that grow use both, each for what it's best at.

Instagram and TikTok are where people **discover** you and get a feel for your brand. Your website is where that interest **converts** — into an enquiry, a booking, a sale — and where Google can actually find you. Social brings the attention; the site turns it into customers. Run one without the other and you leak results: a great feed with nowhere to convert, or a solid site nobody discovers.

If the reason you've held off is cost or hassle, it's smaller than you think. If you have Instagram and nothing else, you have a way to be *discovered* — but no reliable way to be *found* on Google, to *own* your audience, or to *convert* cautious buyers. For almost every Singapore business in 2026, a website is still the piece that does those jobs, working alongside your social, not instead of it.
