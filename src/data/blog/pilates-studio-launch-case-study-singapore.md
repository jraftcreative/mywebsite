Most new fitness studios open to an empty room. The lease is signed, the reformers are installed, the instructors are hired — and on opening day a handful of curious walk-ins trickle through while the founder quietly does the maths on how many months of runway are left.

It doesn't have to go that way. PIKE SIX, a new reformer pilates studio in Singapore, came to Jraft Creative before opening with a different plan: fill the studio *before* the doors open. Here's exactly what we did — and what just the **first 19 days** returned, with the studio still to open and the campaign still running.

## Opening into a crowded market

A new reformer studio opens in Singapore almost every month. ClassPass makes studio-hopping effortless, so people compare three to five studios before committing to any of them. And a brand-new studio starts with the hardest hand of all — no reviews, no word of mouth, no history a nervous first-timer can lean on.

The hardest class to fill is the one you haven't taught yet. So PIKE SIX's goal wasn't a waitlist of maybes. It was a founding base of *paying* members, ready for day one.

## The plan: one page, two ad channels

We kept it deliberately simple. One [high-converting landing page](https://joinpikesix.com/) built around a single action — claim a founding offer — and two ad channels driving to it:

- **Meta (Facebook and Instagram)** to *create* demand. Nobody searches for a studio that hasn't opened, so we put PIKE SIX in front of the right people across its catchment.
- **Google Search** to *capture* the demand that already existed — people actively looking for reformer pilates in the area.

Every dollar pointed at one outcome: a founding sign-up. Not likes, not reach — sign-ups from people ready to pay.

## What happened in the first 19 days

These numbers are from just the **first 19 days** of the presale — and the campaign is still running, with the studio yet to open. In that opening stretch alone, it produced:

- **269 registrations**
- **89 paying founding members** (of 100 founding places)
- **33%** of registrations converted to paying members
- **~S$17** in ad cost per paying member
- **2.9× return on ad spend**, from the founding pack alone

All of that in the first 19 days, before a single class was taught — and the presale is still going as the studio counts down to opening day. That 2.9× return counts only the one-time founding pack, so it's the floor: every one of those members' ongoing membership builds on it from there.

## Why it's working

**The cost per lead was a fraction of the norm.** On Meta, each lead came in at **S$3.85** — against a S$13–33 average for boutique fitness in Singapore, three to nine times lower. Google ran higher at S$21.39 per lead, but that's still well below its S$60–110 norm for this kind of search.

**Each channel did the job it's actually good at.** Meta built demand where none existed yet; Google caught the high-intent searchers. Trying to make one channel do both is where most studio launches leak money.

**The offer was worth acting on, and the scarcity was real.** A hundred founding places, not an open-ended "sign up whenever." When 89 were gone, the pressure to claim the rest was genuine, not manufactured.

**The page was built to convert, not to look pretty.** One action, no distractions, everything a wary first-timer needs to feel safe putting money down before the studio had opened.

**We optimised toward paying members, not cheap sign-ups.** Once real payment data came in, we fed it back to the platforms so they found more people who actually join — not just people who fill in a form.

## What this means for your studio

The specifics were PIKE SIX's — the catchment, the offer, the creative. But the shape applies to any [pilates studio](/pilates-marketing-singapore/), gym, or [fitness business](/industries/fitness/) opening in Singapore.

The lesson is simple: you don't open to an empty room by accident, and you don't fill one by accident either. A studio that markets before opening day starts with members, revenue, and the first handful of reviews that make the next hundred sign-ups far easier to win.
