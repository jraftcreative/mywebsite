In July, a brow embroidery studio in a Singapore heartland estate got 33 enquiries from its ads. In September it got 108.

Same studio, same team, same service. What changed was how the ads were run. Here's what Jraft Creative did, so you can judge what might apply to your own beauty business.

## The starting point

The studio had a loyal base of regulars and plenty of word of mouth. What it didn't have was a steady flow of new clients. Some weeks were full, others were quiet, and there was no reliable way to fill the quiet ones.

Brow embroidery is a considered purchase. People think about it for weeks and want to feel they can trust the artist before they book. Any marketing had to reach them during that thinking time and make enquiring easy.

## What we changed

### Meta ads built for enquiries

We ran Facebook and Instagram ads set up for one job: getting people nearby to enquire. Not likes, not follows. Every ad pointed at the same action, and every setting was chosen with that action in mind. Meta became the main source of new enquiries.

### Google Search, tightened up

We ran Google Search ads alongside Meta to catch people who were already looking for the service. We tightened the keywords, added a long list of searches to exclude, and corrected the location settings. The cost per click came down from about **S$7 to around S$2.30**, roughly a two-thirds cut, so every dollar on Google went further.

### Guarding the account

Google regularly suggests "recommendations": raise the budget, switch on broad matching, add display placements. During one two-day spell, every recommendation was accepted at once, and enquiries stopped. We spotted it in our checks, switched them back off, and results returned. Those suggestions are written to increase spend, not necessarily results, and part of managing an account is knowing which ones to ignore.

## The results

| Month | Enquiries |
|---|---|
| July | 33 |
| August | 86 |
| September | 108 |
| October | 95 |

That's more than three times as many enquiries in September as in July. September came in at about S$15 per enquiry across all ads, and in October Meta brought in 79 enquiries at around **S$12.57 each**. The best single week so far was the week to 5 November: 28 enquiries from Meta at about **S$9.15 each**.

## How we ran it week to week

None of this came from a single clever setting. It came from routine.

Every week we looked at the same few numbers: how many enquiries came in, what each one cost, and which ads were pulling their weight. Ads that brought enquiries kept their budget. Ads that didn't were paused, and the budget moved to the ones that worked. On Google, we read the actual searches people typed before their click and excluded the ones that would never turn into a booking.

The studio got a short report twice a month in plain language: what happened, what we changed, and what we'd watch next. That rhythm is why problems, like the recommendation spell above, were caught in days rather than weeks. For a small business, that's the difference between a bad week and a bad month.

## What any Singapore beauty business can take from this

- **Point every ad at one action**: an enquiry, not likes.
- **Use Meta and Google for different jobs.** One reaches people before they search, the other catches them when they do.
- **Tighten your Google keywords.** Excluding the wrong searches lowers what you pay for the right ones.
- **Treat Google's recommendations with caution.** Read each one before you click "apply".
- **Keep checking.** Accounts drift, and the fastest wins come from catching problems early.
- **Reply fast.** An enquiry is only worth something if someone answers it while the person is still interested.
