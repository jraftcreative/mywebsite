## The Short Answer

If your website gets visitors but almost no enquiries, you have a **conversion problem, not a traffic problem** — and that's actually good news, because conversion is cheaper and faster to fix than traffic. The people are already arriving. Something between the visit and the enquiry is quietly letting them leave.

In almost every case we audit, it's one of seven gaps: an unclear offer, a buried call-to-action, too much friction to get in touch, a slow or confusing mobile experience, missing trust, the wrong kind of traffic, or — surprisingly often — leads that *are* coming in but aren't being tracked. Here's how to find which one is costing you.

## First: Is It Really the Wrong Problem?

Before changing anything, check two numbers so you fix the right thing:

- **If you have steady visitors but a visit-to-enquiry rate well under ~2%**, it's a conversion problem — the page isn't doing its job.
- **If you have barely any visitors at all**, it's a traffic problem (SEO, ads, awareness) — a different fix entirely.

Most SMBs who say "traffic but no leads" genuinely have the first problem: enough people are landing, and the page is leaking them. The rest of this is about closing that leak.

## Gap 1: The One Action Isn't Obvious

A visitor should understand what you want them to do within about five seconds of landing — and there should be **one** clear thing, not five competing ones. If your call-to-action is buried at the bottom, blends into the design, or there are so many links that nothing stands out, people leave without acting.

The fix: one clear, visible call-to-action above the fold ("Book a free consultation," "Get a quote," "WhatsApp us"), repeated at natural decision points down the page. One primary action, stated plainly.

## Gap 2: It's Too Hard to Get in Touch

Every extra step between "interested" and "in touch" loses people. A ten-field contact form, a form as the *only* option, or a contact method hidden on a separate page all cost you leads.

The fix: cut the form to the essentials (name, contact, one line about what they need) and — this matters in Singapore — **add a WhatsApp option.** Singaporeans overwhelmingly prefer messaging to filling forms, and a WhatsApp button often out-converts a form several times over. Make contacting you feel like texting a friend, not submitting paperwork.

## Gap 3: The Page Is Slow or Confusing on Mobile

Most of your traffic is on a phone. If the page loads slowly, text is cramped, buttons are hard to tap, or the layout is confusing on mobile, people bounce before they ever see your offer — and you never find out why. A great desktop site that falls apart on mobile is a leaking site.

The fix: test the real mobile experience — speed, readability, tappable buttons, a clear path to contact. [What a strong business website actually needs](/blog/good-business-website-singapore/) is a useful checklist here.

## Gap 4: There's No Reason to Trust You

A first-time visitor doesn't know you. If the site looks generic or templated, has no reviews, no real photos, and no proof you've done this before, the natural response is to hesitate — and a hesitating visitor leaves to "think about it," which usually means never.

The fix: add genuine trust signals — real client reviews, authentic photos, recognisable logos, a clear sense of who's behind the business. Trust is often the difference between a visit and an enquiry, especially for higher-value services.

## Gap 5: The Offer Isn't Clear or Compelling

Sometimes the page works fine, but the *offer* is vague. "We provide quality services" gives no reason to act now. If a visitor can't quickly tell what they get, who it's for, and why you, they won't convert no matter how clean the design is.

The fix: lead with a specific, concrete offer and the outcome it delivers. Clarity beats cleverness every time — say exactly what you do and what the next step gets them.

## Gap 6: You're Driving the Wrong Traffic

If your visitors are coming from ads or search terms that attract browsers rather than buyers, they'll never convert — because they were never in the market. High traffic with zero leads is sometimes a sign the traffic itself is mismatched to the page.

The fix: check where the traffic comes from and whether it matches buyer intent. If it's paid, the ad and the page have to tell one continuous story — a mismatch there is a common, expensive leak, which we cover in [why your landing page is wasting your ad budget](/blog/landing-page-ads-wasting-money/). Our [paid ads](/services/paid-ads/) work starts from exactly this alignment.

## Gap 7: You're Getting Leads and Don't Know It

This is the one people miss most. If your enquiries arrive by WhatsApp, phone call, or a form that isn't tracked, your analytics may simply **not be recording them** — so it looks like "no leads" when the real problem is "no visibility." We've seen businesses convinced their site was broken when it was quietly working; the tracking was the only thing broken.

The fix: before concluding the page doesn't convert, confirm every contact path — form, WhatsApp, call, booking — is actually being counted. You can't improve, or even judge, what you can't see.

## The Bottom Line

Traffic without leads is a fixable problem, and usually a cheaper one than it feels. Find the gap — unclear action, too much friction, a weak mobile experience, no trust, a vague offer, the wrong traffic, or broken tracking — and close it. You'll get more leads from the visitors you already have, without spending another cent driving more.
